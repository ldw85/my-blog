Ask anyone who's sat through back-to-back meetings, client calls, or interviews: the hard part was never *listening*. It was writing everything down while still being present — and then reconstructing a clean summary afterward. The **Plaud Note Pro** is a $189, credit-card-sized recorder that sticks to the back of your phone, captures the conversation, then uses cloud AI to transcribe it in 112 languages and turn it into structured notes, action items, and mind maps.

## 1. Design & Hardware: Thin Enough to Forget, Sturdy Enough to Trust

The headline number is 0.12 inches (2.99 mm) thin and 1.06 ounces (30 g) — barely more than a credit card. The aluminum body with its textured wave finish feels premium, not plasticky, and the small **AMOLED "InstantView" display** shows recording status, mode, and battery at a glance so you're never guessing whether it's on. Unlike the base Plaud Note, which relies on a colored LED, the Pro's screen is a real quality-of-life upgrade.

It ships with a magnetic case, a magnetic ring for phones without MagSafe, and a charging cable. iPhones snap on and stay put — the whole setup is no thicker than a MagSafe wallet, so it doesn't change how the phone feels in hand. Android users get the same experience via the included adhesive ring. It also works with **Apple Find My**, which is the kind of feature you don't appreciate until you've left a $189 gadget on a conference table.

One genuine annoyance: the charger is a **proprietary magnetic cable, not USB-C**. It keeps the device impossibly thin, but it's one more cable to lose, and a replacement isn't at the nearest gas station.

## 2. Recording Quality: Four Mics and One Clever Trick

The Pro packs **four MEMS microphones plus a bone-conduction VPU**, with AI beamforming that lifts voices from up to **16.4 ft (5 m)** — roughly double the base model's range. In practice, that means a mid-sized conference room recorded from a single point on the table comes back intelligible, including the person at the far end.

The clever part is **smart dual-mode recording**. The device senses whether you're on a phone call or in a live conversation and switches modes automatically — no manual toggle, no menu diving. Stick it to the phone for a call and it switches to call mode; set it on the table and it knows to use meeting mode.

For phone calls it uses **vibration conduction** to capture both sides of the conversation — a genuinely rare trick for iPhone users, since iOS doesn't natively allow call recording. You don't even need speakerphone. There are limits: you can't record while using a headset, and background voices can muddy the transcription.

The honest caveat: the sound is **processed for transcription, not for listening pleasure**. Ambient noise is aggressively suppressed and voice frequencies emphasized, so clarity wins over natural-sounding audio.

## 3. Transcription & Summaries: Where the Magic (and the Subscription) Lives

The Plaud app transcribes in **112 languages with speaker labels**, so group conversations come back as "Sarah: budget is approved" rather than an undifferentiated wall of text. Three features genuinely stand out:

- **Press-to-Highlight.** During a recording, a single short press flags an important moment. The AI then weighs those moments more heavily in the summary and pins them as highlights.
- **Multimodal input.** You can photograph a whiteboard, a client brief, or handwritten notes — or type a quick idea — and it's timestamped into the recording and folded into the generated notes.
- **Ask Plaud.** This is the sleeper feature. After you build up a library of recordings, you can ask questions ("What did the client say about the contract date?") and get answers grounded in your actual recordings, with references to the relevant moments.

## 4. Battery, Storage & Sync

Battery life is a genuine differentiator: **30 hours of continuous recording in Enhance mode** or **50 hours in Endurance mode**, with 60 days of standby. Storage is 64 GB — roughly 480 hours of audio.

The workflow: files sync to your phone in the background over Bluetooth/Wi-Fi, and you access, edit, and export them in the app or web/desktop clients. There's **no direct USB file access** — the device is a capture tool, not a drive.

## 5. Privacy & Security: Built for Sensitive Conversations

Plaud leans hard into compliance: **ISO 27001/27701, SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, and EN18031**. Local recordings are encrypted, cloud files are exclusive to you, and data processing happens only with your explicit authorization.

The nuance: transcription and summaries run in the cloud, so your audio leaves the device even though it's encrypted in transit and at rest. A firm that can't send client audio to any third-party server should treat cloud processing as a consideration.

One US-specific note: call-recording consent rules vary by state, and a number of states require all-party consent. Get consent before recording conversations.

## 6. Pricing: The $189 Device Is Half the Story

The hardware is $189, but the AI engine runs on a subscription:

- **Starter — free:** 300 transcription minutes/month. Fine for light use.
- **Pro — $17.99/mo or $99.99/yr:** 1,200 minutes/month. The realistic choice for regular meeting-goers.
- **Unlimited — $29.99/mo or $239.99/yr:** unlimited transcription.

One subscription covers every Plaud device you own. Still, be honest with yourself: a year of Pro adds about $100 to the $189 device — roughly $289 all-in for year one.

## 7. Who Should Buy It — and Who Should Skip

**Buy it if you:**

- Record 3+ meetings, client calls, or interviews a week — especially in-person or over the phone
- Are a journalist, consultant, salesperson, attorney, clinician, or researcher who needs accurate notes and quotes
- Want a searchable memory of conversations and ask questions about them later (Ask Plaud)
- Need discreet, offline recording that doesn't drain your phone
- Handle sensitive information and want documented compliance (HIPAA, SOC 2, ISO 27001)

**Skip it if you:**

- Mostly take virtual meetings where bots already transcribe for free
- Record a couple of times a month — the free 300 minutes and a phone app will do
- Refuse subscriptions on principle — the AI features are the product, and they're metered
- Need audiophile-quality recordings — this is tuned for transcription

But if you've ever spent an evening reconstructing what was decided in a meeting you were actually in, this is the gadget that ends that era.

## FAQ: Plaud Note Pro

**Does the Plaud Note Pro work without a subscription?**

Yes, but with limits. The device records, stores, and syncs fine with no paid plan, and the free Starter plan includes 300 transcription minutes per month. Everything AI (transcription, summaries, Ask Plaud) is the metered part.

**Can it transcribe in real time?**

No. You record first, the audio syncs to the app, and the transcript and summary are generated afterward — usually within minutes.

**Can it record phone calls?**

Yes. Attached to your phone, it uses vibration conduction to capture both sides of a call — no speakerphone needed, and it works on iPhone. You can't use a headset while recording, and call-recording consent laws vary by state.
